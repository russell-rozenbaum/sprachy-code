# Incidental German correction in coding chats: research notes

This is the research basis for `skills/sprachy/references/method.md` and `languages/*/method.md`. Coverage runs from 2020–2026, plus foundational work. Compiled 2026-09-29.

## 1. Oral and interactional corrective feedback
- **Lyster & Saito (2010):** a meta-analysis of 15 classroom studies (N=827). Corrective feedback has significant, durable effects. **Prompts beat recasts.** Prompts are elicitation, metalinguistic clues, clarification requests and repetition. A recast simply restates the sentence correctly. The largest effects were on free-production measures. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ892626
- **Li (2010), meta-analysis:** feedback has a medium effect that lasts over time. Explicit feedback shows larger immediate effects; implicit feedback is more durable. Delayed effect sizes: prompts d≈1.14, recasts 0.70, explicit correction 0.60. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00561.x
- **Ammar & Spada (2006):** low-proficiency learners gain much more from prompts. High-proficiency learners gain equally from recasts, because they notice them. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ777400
- **Yang & Lyster (2010):** for rule-based (regular) morphology, prompts beat recasts. For irregular, item-based forms, the two are equal. A multisite replication (Language Learning, 2023) is at https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/lang.12623

## 2. Written corrective feedback
- **Sheen (2007):** direct correction with a short metalinguistic note beat direct correction alone on delayed tests.
- **Bitchener & Knoch (2010):** focused feedback (1–2 targets) produced gains that lasted months. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1279343
- **Kang & Han (2015), meta-analysis:** written feedback improves accuracy, and **proficiency is the strongest moderator**. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/modl.12189
- **Lim & Renandya (2020), meta-analysis of 35 studies:** confirms that written feedback improves grammatical accuracy. https://tesl-ej.org/wordpress/issues/volume24/ej95/ej95a3/
- **Truscott (1996)** argued that grammar correction doesn't work. That debate is why we cap corrections instead of correcting everything.
- **Direct correction** suits lower levels and "untreatable" errors such as vocabulary and gender. **Indirect correction** suits rule-governed errors at higher levels (Ferris 2006; Bitchener 2012).

## 3. Theory
- **Noticing (Schmidt 1990):** only what the learner notices becomes learning. That is why we bold the changed part.
- **Output (Swain 1985/1995):** learners progress by being pushed to produce language. This supports micro-lessons and occasional prompts.
- **Interaction (Long 1996; Mackey & Goo 2007):** conversation plus feedback promotes acquisition.
- **Comprehensible input i+1 (Krashen 1982):** input should be just above the learner's current level.
- **Processability Theory (Pienemann 1998):** German word order develops in a fixed sequence: SVO → adverb fronting → verb separation → inversion/V2 → verb-final in subordinate clauses. Teaching works only for the *next* stage.
- **Diehl et al. (2000), DiGS:** case develops late and slowly (nominative → accusative → dative).

## 4. Translanguaging
- **Wang & Zhang (2026), Language Teaching, Bayesian meta-analysis of 40 studies (N=3,145):** g≈1.01 overall. The adult non-formal subgroup was much lower, g≈0.26. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/multilevel-bayesian-metaanalysis-of-the-effects-of-translanguaging-pedagogy-on-l2-achievement/552777E82223C539135A50D9B1DAADB4
- García & Li Wei (2014) and Cummins' interdependence hypothesis treat the first language as a resource.

## 5. Spacing, retrieval and memory
- **Kim & Webb (2022), meta-analysis of 48 experiments:** spaced practice beats massed practice, g≈1.15 on delayed tests. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/lang.12479
- **Karpicke & Roediger (2008):** retrieving an item is what drives retention. **Cepeda et al. (2006/2008):** the optimal gap grows with how long you need to remember.
- **Settles & Meeder (2016):** Duolingo's half-life regression model.
- **LOOM (Cui, Pu & Grossman 2025):** builds a learner memory graph from everyday LLM chats. Users wanted more control. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21037

## 6. Affect and motivation
- Krashen's affective filter hypothesis; Dewaele & MacIntyre (2014) on enjoyment.
- **Kluger & DeNisi (1996):** about a third of feedback interventions *hurt* performance, mostly when feedback targets the self rather than the task.
- **Schulz (2001):** adult learners want to be corrected.
- **Kamelabad (2025), LLM chatbot:** immediate and delayed feedback gave the same learning gains, but users preferred immediate feedback. https://diva-portal.org/smash/record.jsf?pid=diva2:2043379
- **Hattie & Timperley (2007):** specific praise beats generic praise.

## 7. Common errors English speakers make in German, by priority
1. V2 word order
2. Verb-final order in subordinate clauses
3. Noun gender
4. Case after prepositions and verbs
5. Separable verbs and the verb bracket
6. haben vs. sein in the perfect tense
7. Adjective endings
8. False friends (aktuell, eventuell, also, bekommen, Chef, "Sinn machen")
9. Sie vs. du
10. German-inflected English verbs (gepusht). **Accept these; they are not errors.**

## 8. LLMs as tutors
- **Lyu et al. (2025), meta-analysis of chatbots:** g≈0.61. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ijal.12668
- LLMs make accurate corrections but **over-correct**, so they need to be told to make minimal edits (PLOS One 2024; BEA 2025). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11524451/ · https://aclanthology.org/2025.bea-1.9.pdf
- **Webb, Uchihara & Yanagisawa (2023):** incidental vocabulary gains are real but modest, and they grow with repetition.
- **Habit stacking:** implementation intentions (Gollwitzer 1999, d≈0.65) and Tiny Habits (Fogg 2019). **Not directly studied for language learning. Treat it as a hypothesis and measure it.**

## 9. Adapting feedback to CEFR level
The Council of Europe's CEFR Companion Volume (2020) defines the levels. Lower levels need explicit feedback; higher levels can handle implicit feedback. The level table is in `method.md` §3.

## Caveat
Direct evidence for *incidental* correction embedded in unrelated work is thin. We should log uptake (does the error rate per tag fall over time?) to measure whether this tool actually works.
